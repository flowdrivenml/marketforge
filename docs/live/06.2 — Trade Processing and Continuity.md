## 1. Purpose

The Trade Processing and Continuity subsystem manages the lifecycle of exchange-reported executions after they enter the MarketForge Live processing pipeline.

It transforms normalized trade records into an authoritative logical execution stream while maintaining trade identity, deduplication, continuity diagnostics, and consistent publication ordering.

Unlike L2 processing, trade processing does not maintain a reconstructed market state.

Each trade represents an independent exchange-reported execution or aggregate execution record.

However, **the execution stream itself has stateful integrity requirements**, particularly during reconnection, recovery, replay, and subscription replacement.

The subsystem must be:

- Exchange-agnostic.
- Configuration-driven.
- Deterministic.
- High-throughput.
- Generation-safe.
- Memory-bounded.
- Compatible with the existing MarketForge historical processing architecture.

### 1.1 Supported Scope

The initial implementation supports five exchanges:

- Binance.
- Bybit.
- OKX.
- Bitget.
- Gate.io.

Across three market categories:

- Spot.
- Linear.
- Inverse.

The subsystem processes the trade channels defined in:

    data/.config/live/formats/trades/

The authoritative connectivity and native trade-format specifications are documented in **04 — Exchange Protocols and Connectivity**.

### 1.2 Primary Objectives

The subsystem must:

- Accept normalized exchange-native trade records.
- Preserve individual versus aggregated trade semantics.
- Maintain stable execution identities.
- Detect exact and conflicting duplicates.
- Track trade-stream coverage.
- Identify suspected and confirmed discontinuities.
- Coordinate live ingestion during recovery.
- Reconcile recovered and live executions.
- Preserve source ordering and event timestamps.
- Assign canonical publication identities.
- Prevent duplicate canonical publication.
- Report unresolved coverage gaps.
- Maintain bounded processing state.
- Expose trade-stream health and diagnostics.

The subsystem must not invent missing executions or claim completeness without supporting evidence.

### 1.3 Fundamental Distinction from L2

L2 processing reconstructs state:

    Snapshot
        |
        v
    Delta
        |
        v
    Delta
        |
        v
    Current Order Book

Trade processing maintains a sequence of independent events:

    Trade A
       |
       v
    Trade B
       |
       v
    Trade C

A trade does not require previous trades to determine its execution price, aggressor side, or quantity.

Nevertheless, previous events may be required to establish:

- Whether a trade is duplicated.
- Whether native identifiers remain consistent.
- Whether a recovery interval overlaps existing data.
- Whether the acquisition stream has unresolved gaps.
- Whether publication ordering is preserved.

Consequently, trade normalization is predominantly stateless, while trade continuity management is stateful.

### 1.4 Non-Goals

The Trade Processing subsystem does not:

- Maintain an in-memory history of every execution.
- Reconstruct an order book.
- Independently establish WebSocket connections.
- Manage exchange subscription admission.
- Implement REST connectivity.
- Own global recovery policy.
- Implement cross-stream event-time merging.
- Construct imbalance bars or run bars.
- Calculate trading signals.
- Write directly to PostgreSQL.
- Manage temporary Parquet retention.

Longer-term event retention belongs to the existing Live buffering and persistence infrastructure.

Trade processing retains only the bounded state necessary for execution integrity and recovery reconciliation.

## 2. Architectural Boundaries

The Trade Processing subsystem operates downstream of the exchange protocol adapter and normalization engine.

It coordinates with the Recovery Coordinator when continuity becomes uncertain.

### 2.1 Component Responsibilities

| Component | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Stream Registry | Logical stream identity and lifecycle |
| Subscription Manager | Native trade subscription management |
| Connection Manager | Physical WebSocket connectivity |
| Exchange Protocol Adapter | Native message routing and classification |
| Trade Normalizer | Decode and normalize execution records |
| Trade Processor | Per-stream execution processing |
| Trade Integrity Validator | Identity, duplicate, and continuity validation |
| Trade Reconciliation Engine | Reconcile live and recovered executions |
| Recovery Coordinator | Recovery scheduling, policy, retries, escalation |
| Publisher | Canonical event distribution |
| Live Retention Manager | Temporary in-memory and Parquet retention |
| Recording Manager | Optional PostgreSQL market-data persistence |
| Metrics Registry | Operational metrics and health |

The Trade Processor must not duplicate responsibilities already owned by these components.

### 2.2 Processing Architecture

    Exchange WebSocket
            |
            v
    Connection Manager
            |
            v
    Exchange Protocol Adapter
            |
            v
    Trade Normalizer
            |
            v
    Trade Processor
            |
            v
    Trade Integrity Validator
            |
            v
    Trade Continuity State
            |
            v
    Canonical Trade Acceptance
            |
            v
    Publisher
            |
       +----+----+
       |         |
       v         v
    Subscribers  Optional Recording
       |
       v
    Downstream Processing

Recovery follows a separate path:

    Recovery Coordinator
             |
             v
    Historical Trade Acquisition
             |
             v
    Trade Normalizer
             |
             v
    Trade Reconciliation Engine
             |
             v
    Canonical Trade Acceptance

Both paths must use compatible normalization and identity semantics.

### 2.3 Ownership Boundaries

The Trade Processor owns:

- Per-stream trade processing state.
- Accepted execution ordering.
- Bounded deduplication state.
- Trade continuity state.
- Integration with canonical publication.
- Processing-generation validation.

The Recovery Coordinator owns:

- Recovery decisions.
- Recovery scheduling.
- REST or archive acquisition coordination.
- Retry policies.
- Recovery escalation.

The Trade Reconciliation Engine owns:

- Comparing recovered and live executions.
- Detecting recovery overlap.
- Removing exact duplicates.
- Identifying conflicting executions.
- Establishing recovery coverage evidence.
- Producing an ordered reconciliation result.

The Publisher owns delivery to downstream consumers.

The Trade Processor does not independently initiate HTTP requests or maintain a separate historical storage system.

### 2.4 Design Constraints

- Rust-only processing hot path.
- No Python calls during event processing.
- No PostgreSQL queries during event processing.
- No exchange-specific trade-processing pipelines.
- No arbitrary assumptions about native trade-ID continuity.
- No silent duplicate publication.
- No silent loss of accepted executions.
- No unbounded recovery buffers.
- No fabricated recovery results.
- No implicit event-time ordering guarantees.
- No acceptance of obsolete subscription generations.
- No incompatible metadata revisions within one atomic record batch.
- No direct dependency on the TUI.
- No direct market-data persistence inside the Trade Processor.

The implementation must reuse the canonical trade contracts and normalization strategies defined in **02 — Domain Models and Contracts** and **06 — Normalization and Integrity**.

## 3. Trade Processing Domain Models

The subsystem maintains a small set of runtime entities.

These describe logical execution streams rather than physical exchange connections.

### 3.1 TradeStreamDescriptor

Immutable logical stream definition.

| Field | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| stream_id | StreamId | Stable logical source-stream identity |
| instrument_id | InstrumentId | Canonical instrument |
| exchange | ExchangeId | Exchange identity |
| market | MarketCategory | Spot, Linear, Inverse |
| native_channel | String | Exchange trade channel |
| trade_semantics | TradeSemantics | Individual or Aggregated |
| format_id | FormatId | Compiled trade format |
| configuration_revision | RevisionId | Effective processing configuration |

The descriptor is associated with the Stream Registry.

It must not contain mutable connection state.

### 3.2 TradeStreamRuntime

Mutable processing state.

Conceptually:

    TradeStreamRuntime {
        stream_id

        connection_generation
        subscription_generation
        processing_generation

        configuration_revision
        metadata_revision

        lifecycle_state
        coverage_state

        last_accepted_trade_identity
        last_accepted_event_timestamp

        deduplication_state
        reconciliation_state

        last_error
    }

This structure is illustrative.

The implementation must reuse existing canonical identifiers and time representations.

### 3.3 ProcessingGeneration

A processing generation identifies one active trade-processing incarnation.

It is associated with:

    StreamId
    ConnectionGeneration
    SubscriptionGeneration
    ProcessingGeneration
    ConfigurationRevision
    MetadataRevision

A processing generation is distinct from a connection generation.

A stream processor may be replaced without changing the logical StreamId.

The generation must prevent obsolete processors or delayed recovery operations from corrupting current state.

### 3.4 Trade Processing States

The Trade Processor maintains an operational state separate from the Stream Registry lifecycle.

Supported states:

    Uninitialized
    Initializing
    Live
    CoverageUnverified
    Reconciling
    Degraded
    Stopping
    Stopped
    Failed

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Uninitialized | Processor has not been prepared |
| Initializing | Context and integrity state being established |
| Live | Current live execution processing is operational |
| CoverageUnverified | Live processing works but completeness is uncertain |
| Reconciling | Recovery results are being reconciled with live executions |
| Degraded | Required integrity or resource guarantees are impaired |
| Stopping | Shutdown or replacement underway |
| Stopped | Processing terminated |
| Failed | Nonrecoverable processing failure |

The Stream Registry remains authoritative for the logical stream lifecycle.

Trade processing state provides more detailed execution-specific diagnostics.

### 3.5 Trade Coverage State

Coverage describes the degree of confidence that the execution stream contains all relevant native trades.

Supported classifications:

    VerifiedContinuous
    UnverifiedCoverage
    SuspectedGap
    ConfirmedGap
    Recovering
    Recovered
    Incomplete

These classifications must be evidence-based.

For example:

    WebSocket Connected

does not imply:

    VerifiedContinuous

Likewise:

    Recovery Request Successful

does not imply:

    Coverage Complete

The exact evidence required for a particular state depends on the native protocol and configured integrity strategy.

### 3.6 TradeStreamCursor

The Trade Processor must preserve a stable relationship between accepted canonical events and their publication order.

Conceptually:

    StreamCursor {
        stream_id
        log_epoch
        offset
    }

A stream cursor is an internal MarketForge ordering identifier.
The authoritative cursor identity is `(StreamId, LogEpoch, offset)`,
as defined in Document 02, Section 4.1.

`LogEpoch` identifies the MarketForge event-log namespace.
It is independent of connection, subscription, processing,
and book generations.

Trade-processor reinitialization or WebSocket reconnection
must not independently reset the cursor offset or allocate
a new log epoch.

A new log epoch is required only when the existing cursor
namespace cannot safely continue or another explicitly
defined log-lifecycle transition requires it.

Each accepted logical trade event receives one cursor.
Rejected or deduplicated native messages do not create
additional accepted-event positions.

It is not equivalent to:

- Native trade ID.
- Exchange sequence ID.
- Event timestamp.
- WebSocket receive sequence.
- REST recovery position.

Cursor semantics must remain consistent with **02 — Domain Models and Contracts**.

### 3.7 Native Execution Identity

The Trade Processor uses the compiled identity strategy defined in document 06.

Conceptually:

    TradeIdentity {
        exchange
        market
        instrument_id
        native_trade_id
        trade_semantics
    }

Additional fields may be included when the native identity scope requires them.

The identity must be stable across compatible WebSocket and recovery sources.

When identity compatibility cannot be established, reconciliation must use an explicitly supported alternative strategy.

## 4. Trade Processor Lifecycle

The Trade Processor is created when a logical trade stream is activated.

Its lifecycle is coordinated with the Stream Registry and Subscription Manager.

### 4.1 Initialization

    Stream Activation
           |
           v
    Resolve Compiled Trade Format
           |
           v
    Resolve Instrument Context
           |
           v
    Initialize Integrity Strategy
           |
           v
    Allocate Bounded State
           |
           v
    Establish Message Routing
           |
           v
    Confirm Processing Readiness
           |
           v
    Begin Live Processing

The processor must be ready to accept routed messages before the native subscription can produce unbuffered executions.

### 4.2 Initialization Requirements

Before processing becomes active:

- Trade format compilation must succeed.
- Instrument metadata must be validated.
- Quantity normalization must be supported.
- Native trade identity must be configured.
- Aggressor-side interpretation must be supported.
- Timestamp conversion must be valid.
- Deduplication capacity must be available.
- Message routing must be established.
- The active subscription generation must be known.

Failure to satisfy these requirements prevents activation.

### 4.3 Live Processing

During normal operation:

    Native Trade Batch
           |
           v
    Validate Generation
           |
           v
    Normalize Records
           |
           v
    Validate Identity
           |
           v
    Check Deduplication State
           |
           v
    Evaluate Continuity
           |
           v
    Accept Canonical Trades
           |
           v
    Publish in Required Order

The processor must preserve record ordering according to the compiled native protocol.

### 4.4 Connection Loss

When the owning WebSocket connection fails:

- The current connection generation becomes invalid.
- In-flight obsolete messages are fenced.
- Trade coverage becomes unverified where completeness cannot be established.
- Relevant deduplication state is retained according to policy.
- The processor reports the interruption boundary.
- Recovery coordination begins when supported and required.

The logical StreamId remains unchanged.

A connection failure must not cause previously accepted trades to be published again.

### 4.5 Subscription Restoration

After reconnection:

    New Connection Generation
             |
             v
    Restore Native Subscription
             |
             v
    Establish Processing Generation
             |
             v
    Resume Live Acquisition
             |
             v
    Evaluate Recovery Requirements

Live acquisition and recovery reconciliation are distinct operations.

The processor must not claim recovered historical continuity merely because native subscription restoration succeeded.

### 4.6 Deactivation

When the user deactivates a trade stream:

- Fence the current processing generation.
- Stop accepting new native messages.
- Resolve or cancel active reconciliation operations.
- Apply the configured shutdown and drain policy.
- Release bounded integrity state when no longer required.
- Report final coverage diagnostics.
- Release processor resources.

The physical WebSocket connection may remain active for other subscriptions.

### 4.7 Removal

Removal permanently releases the logical trade-processing registration.

Late acknowledgements, delayed recovered records, or obsolete processing completions must not recreate the removed processor.

Removal must be idempotent.

## 5. Execution Identity Management

Execution identity is the foundation of trade deduplication, recovery reconciliation, and continuity tracking.

MarketForge must preserve the identity assigned by the exchange without introducing artificial assumptions about its structure or ordering.

Trade identity semantics are compiled from the exchange-specific trade format.

The Trade Processor must not contain hardcoded identity rules for individual exchanges.

### 5.1 Identity Responsibilities

The execution identity subsystem must:

- Extract native trade identifiers.
- Preserve identifiers without precision loss.
- Respect exchange-defined identity scopes.
- Distinguish individual and aggregated executions.
- Construct deterministic deduplication keys.
- Detect repeated execution identities.
- Identify conflicting duplicates.
- Support live and recovered execution reconciliation.
- Preserve supplementary native identifiers.
- Avoid inferring missing executions from unsupported identifier assumptions.

The detailed normalization rules remain defined in **06 — Normalization and Integrity**.

This section defines the runtime identity-management behavior.

### 5.2 TradeIdentity

Conceptual representation:

    TradeIdentity {
        exchange
        market
        instrument_id
        native_trade_id
        trade_semantics
    }

Additional fields may be required for exchange-specific identity scopes.

Examples include:

    NativeChannel
    TradingSession
    AggregationType

The Configuration Compiler determines the required identity components.

A trade identity must remain stable across compatible live and recovery sources.

### 5.3 Identity Representation

Native trade identifiers may use:

    UnsignedInteger
    IntegerString
    OpaqueString

Identifiers must not be converted through floating-point arithmetic.

For example:

    "12345678901234567890"

must retain its exact value.

An opaque identifier must not be interpreted as a sequential integer merely because it contains digits.

### 5.4 Identity Namespaces

Native identifiers may be unique within:

- One instrument.
- One market.
- One exchange.
- One native channel.
- One trading session.
- One aggregation namespace.

For example:

    Individual Trade ID = 100

and:

    Aggregate Trade ID = 100

do not necessarily identify the same execution.

The canonical identity must preserve the native namespace.

### 5.5 Identity Across Subscription Generations

A trade's economic identity must remain independent of transport generations.

For example:

    Subscription Generation 5
        Trade ID 100

    Subscription Generation 6
        Trade ID 100

If both records refer to the same native execution, they must resolve to the same deduplication identity.

Therefore, the following are provenance fields rather than default deduplication-key components:

    ConnectionGeneration
    SubscriptionGeneration
    ProcessingGeneration

Including them in the deduplication key would prevent correct duplicate detection across reconnects.

### 5.6 Identity Across Recovery Sources

Recovery records may originate from:

- Public REST trade endpoints.
- Exchange historical archives.
- Existing MarketForge historical datasets.
- Other explicitly supported acquisition sources.

These records must be mapped into a compatible identity namespace before reconciliation.

The engine must not assume WebSocket and REST identifiers are equivalent merely because their field names match.

Identity compatibility must be established through protocol documentation and recorded fixtures.

### 5.7 Missing Native Identity

When a native record lacks the identifier required by its compiled format:

    MissingTradeIdentity

The processor must follow the configured identity policy.

The initial conservative policy is:

    RejectOrQuarantine

The processor must not fabricate a native execution identifier from:

- Event timestamp.
- Execution price.
- Executed quantity.
- Aggressor side.
- WebSocket receive position.

Such values may be used for explicitly defined diagnostic fingerprints, but they do not establish exchange-native identity.

## 6. Deduplication State

The Trade Processor maintains bounded state to detect repeated native executions.

This state is independent of market-data recording and temporary historical retention.

### 6.1 Deduplication Requirements

The deduplication mechanism must support:
 
- Fast identity lookup.
- Exact duplicate detection.
- Conflicting duplicate detection.
- Bounded memory consumption.
- Configurable retention.
- Reconnection overlap.
- Recovery overlap.
- Deterministic eviction.
- Explicit uncertainty outside the retained identity window.

It must not store every trade indefinitely.

### 6.2 DeduplicationEntry

Conceptual representation:

    DeduplicationEntry {
        trade_identity
        trade_fingerprint
        first_accepted_cursor
        first_accepted_at
        event_timestamp
    }

The exact representation may be optimized to reduce memory usage.

The entry records enough information to identify a repeated execution and distinguish an exact duplicate from a conflict.

### 6.3 DeduplicationKey

The key is constructed using the compiled native identity strategy.

Conceptually:

    DeduplicationKey =
        Exchange
        + Market
        + InstrumentId
        + NativeTradeId
        + TradeSemantics

The identity scope may include additional protocol-specific components.

The key must not include connection generation merely to distinguish reconnects.

### 6.4 TradeFingerprint

A fingerprint represents the economically relevant content associated with one native trade identity.

Conceptually:

    TradeFingerprint {
        price
        base_quantity
        quote_quantity
        aggressor_side
        event_timestamp
        trade_semantics
    }

The fingerprint must use deterministic canonical representations.

Numerically equivalent decimals must compare according to their economic value.

For example:

    1.0

and:

    1.00

represent the same quantity.

The fingerprint may additionally include exchange-specific fields when they are necessary to identify a genuine execution conflict.

### 6.5 Duplicate Classification

An incoming trade produces one of:

    NewExecution
    ExactDuplicate
    ConflictingDuplicate
    IdentityUnavailable

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| NewExecution | Identity has not previously been accepted within the verified identity state |
| ExactDuplicate | Identity and relevant execution content match |
| ConflictingDuplicate | Identity matches but execution content differs |
| IdentityUnavailable | Required identity cannot be established |

A cache miss alone does not prove that an execution has never been published.

It only establishes that the identity is absent from the currently available deduplication state.

### 6.6 Exact Duplicates

An exact duplicate must not be published again through the normal live event path.

Required behavior:

    Incoming Trade
          |
          v
    Identity Lookup
          |
          v
    Existing Identity
          |
          v
    Compare Fingerprint
          |
          v
    Exact Match
          |
          v
    Suppress Duplicate Publication

The duplicate may increment diagnostic counters.

It must not receive a new accepted canonical-event cursor.

### 6.7 Conflicting Duplicates

A conflicting duplicate occurs when the same native identity appears with different economically relevant content.

Example:

    Trade ID: 100

    First accepted:
        price = 65000
        quantity = 0.5

    Incoming:
        price = 65010
        quantity = 0.5

The processor must report:

    TradeIdentityConflict

It must not silently overwrite the previously accepted execution.

Possible actions include:

- Quarantine the incoming record.
- Mark coverage as degraded.
- Request reconciliation.
- Escalate repeated conflicts.

The Recovery Coordinator determines whether an independent source can resolve the conflict.

### 6.8 Deduplication Cache

The initial implementation should use a bounded hash-based identity index.

Conceptually:

    HashMap<TradeIdentity, DeduplicationEntry>

An additional bounded eviction structure may maintain retention order.

Possible policies:

    MaximumEntries
    MaximumAge
    MaximumMemoryBytes

The implementation must not perform an unbounded scan for every incoming trade.

### 6.9 Cache Eviction

Eviction removes old identities from memory.

Eviction must not imply that the corresponding executions have become new.

The system must distinguish:

    IdentityNotPresentInCache

from:

    IdentityNeverPublished

For ordinary live operation, an evicted identity may no longer be detectable as a duplicate using memory alone.

For recovery intervals exceeding the retained window, reconciliation must use a sufficiently broad identity source or explicitly report that complete deduplication cannot be guaranteed.

### 6.10 Reconnection Retention

A connection failure must not automatically clear deduplication state.

The processor should retain relevant identities across compatible subscription generations.

Example:

    Before disconnect:
        IDs 1000–1100 accepted

    After reconnect:
        IDs 1095–1120 received

The overlap:

    1095–1100

must be recognized when the native identity rules and retained state permit it.

The new executions:

    1101–1120

may then be accepted according to the continuity policy.

### 6.11 Metadata Revisions and Fingerprints

An execution may be normalized under different instrument metadata revisions during recovery.

A fingerprint comparison must not incorrectly classify the same native execution as conflicting merely because derived quantities were calculated using different metadata.

The reconciliation procedure must compare compatible economic representations.

When normalization revisions are incompatible, the engine must resolve the applicable metadata revision or report an unresolved normalization conflict.

It must not silently choose the newest revision.

## 7. Trade Continuity Tracking

Trade continuity describes whether the acquired execution stream contains the complete native trade history for a relevant interval.

Many public trade feeds do not provide sufficient sequencing guarantees to prove completeness directly.

Therefore, MarketForge must distinguish continuity evidence from operational connectivity.

### 7.1 Continuity Responsibilities

The Trade Continuity Tracker must:

- Record accepted execution boundaries.
- Track acquisition interruptions.
- Preserve native identifier diagnostics.
- Identify suspicious discontinuities.
- Detect confirmed gaps where supported.
- Record unresolved coverage intervals.
- Coordinate recovery requests.
- Incorporate recovery evidence.
- Expose coverage status to downstream consumers.

The tracker must not independently execute REST requests.

### 7.2 CoverageState

Supported states:

    VerifiedContinuous
    UnverifiedCoverage
    SuspectedGap
    ConfirmedGap
    Recovering
    Recovered
    Incomplete

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| VerifiedContinuous | Supported evidence establishes continuity for a defined interval |
| UnverifiedCoverage | Completeness cannot currently be established |
| SuspectedGap | Evidence suggests missing trades but is inconclusive |
| ConfirmedGap | A supported continuity rule proves missing coverage |
| Recovering | Recovery is being attempted |
| Recovered | Required recovery coverage has been verified |
| Incomplete | One or more known or unresolved coverage gaps remain |

The state is associated with an explicit interval.

It must not be interpreted as a permanent guarantee covering the entire lifetime of the instrument.

### 7.3 CoverageInterval

Conceptual representation:

    CoverageInterval {
        stream_id

        start_timestamp
        end_timestamp

        first_native_identity
        last_native_identity

        coverage_state
        evidence
        source
    }

The interval boundaries must distinguish exact known boundaries from estimated boundaries.

For example, the last accepted execution before a disconnect and the first accepted execution after reconnection provide useful bounds.

They do not necessarily identify the exact timestamps of missing executions.

### 7.4 Continuity Evidence

Continuity may be supported by:

- Verified native consecutive identifiers.
- Exchange-defined sequence rules.
- Complete paginated historical retrieval.
- Historical archives with documented coverage.
- Reconciliation against independently acquired executions.
- Verified overlap between live and recovered intervals.

The amount of evidence required depends on the exchange and channel.

A successful API response is not by itself sufficient.

### 7.5 Increasing vs Consecutive IDs

Some exchanges document increasing trade identifiers.

However:

    Increasing != Consecutive

Example:

    Previous ID = 100

    Current ID = 105

This does not necessarily mean four executions were lost.

The exchange may allocate identifiers according to a broader namespace or another documented rule.

The processor must not calculate exact missing-trade counts without a verified consecutive-ID guarantee.

### 7.6 Opaque Identifiers

Opaque native identifiers provide identity but may not support numerical continuity analysis.

For example:

    execId = "a8f3..."

The processor may use the identifier for deduplication.

It must not infer ordering or missing executions from its lexical value.

### 7.7 Timestamp Discontinuities

A large interval between accepted trade timestamps does not automatically indicate data loss.

Example:

    Trade A at 12:00:00

    Trade B at 12:00:15

A low-activity instrument may legitimately have no executions during those 15 seconds.

Therefore:

    Time Gap != Confirmed Trade Loss

Timestamp discontinuities may trigger diagnostics or reconciliation under a configured policy.

They must not independently establish missing execution counts.

## 8. Acquisition Interruption Boundaries

Transport and subscription interruptions create uncertainty about execution coverage.

The Trade Processor must record these boundaries explicitly.

### 8.1 Interruption Triggers

Potential triggers include:

- WebSocket disconnection.
- Subscription deactivation.
- Subscription replacement.
- Connection replacement.
- Processor restart.
- Message-routing failure.
- Inbound queue overflow.
- Normalization failure affecting execution completeness.
- Runtime crash or unexpected termination.

The coverage consequences depend on whether events may have occurred while acquisition was unavailable.

### 8.2 InterruptionBoundary

Conceptual representation:

    InterruptionBoundary {
        stream_id

        connection_generation
        subscription_generation
        processing_generation

        last_accepted_trade_identity
        last_accepted_event_timestamp
        last_received_timestamp

        interruption_detected_at
        reason
    }

The boundary must preserve enough information to support recovery planning.

### 8.3 Connection Loss

Example:

    Trade ID 1000 accepted
             |
             v
    WebSocket Connection Lost
             |
             v
    Coverage Becomes Unverified
             |
             v
    Connection Restored
             |
             v
    Trade ID 1030 received

The processor must not assume that executions 1001–1029 were missed unless the native identity rules prove consecutiveness.

Nevertheless, the disconnected interval remains a legitimate recovery target.

### 8.4 Processor Failure

A processor may fail while the physical WebSocket remains connected.

For example:

    WebSocket Receiving
             |
             v
    Trade Processor Fails
             |
             v
    Inbound Queue Accumulates
             |
             v
    Queue Capacity Exceeded

If the accepted processing pipeline can no longer preserve all incoming records, continuity becomes uncertain.

The processor must report the failure even though transport connectivity remains healthy.

### 8.5 Subscription Replacement

A controlled subscription replacement may briefly overlap old and new generations.

The Trade Processor must reconcile executions received through both generations.

The replacement must not result in:

- Duplicate publication.
- Silent loss of executions.
- Incompatible normalization revisions.
- Untracked coverage gaps.

If continuity cannot be established, the interval remains unverified.

### 8.6 Intentional Deactivation

An intentionally inactive stream has no obligation to acquire executions during its inactive period.

However, if a later consumer requests a historically continuous dataset spanning that interval, the missing acquisition period must remain identifiable.

Intentional inactivity must not be silently represented as continuous market-data coverage.

### 8.7 Interruption Reporting

An interruption event should include:

    StreamId
    LastAcceptedCursor
    LastNativeTradeIdentity
    LastEventTimestamp
    InterruptionStart
    InterruptionEnd
    FailureReason
    CoverageClassification

The exact interruption interval may be uncertain.

The system must not fabricate precise missing-execution boundaries.

## 9. Continuity State Transitions

Coverage state changes must be driven by explicit evidence and recovery outcomes.

### 9.1 Normal Live Processing

When the exchange provides sufficient continuity guarantees:

    VerifiedContinuous
            |
            v
    Continue Valid Processing
            |
            v
    VerifiedContinuous

For feeds without complete native continuity guarantees, normal operation may instead remain:

    UnverifiedCoverage

This is not necessarily a stream failure.

It reflects the limits of the available evidence.

### 9.2 Suspected Gap

A suspicious condition may trigger:

    UnverifiedCoverage
            |
            v
       SuspectedGap
            |
            v
    Request Reconciliation

Examples include:

- Unexpected native ID regression.
- Unexplained timestamp discontinuity.
- Unusual message-rate collapse.
- Conflicting trade identities.
- Subscription interruption.

A suspected gap must not be counted as confirmed missing executions.

### 9.3 Confirmed Gap

A confirmed gap requires verified evidence.

Example:

    Native protocol guarantees consecutive IDs.

    Last accepted ID = 1000

    Next accepted ID = 1005

Under that verified guarantee, the missing range may be identified.

The processor may report:

    ConfirmedGap

The recovery mechanism then attempts to retrieve the missing executions.

### 9.4 Recovery Transition

Conceptually:

    Gap Detected
          |
          v
    Recovering
          |
          v
    Reconciliation Result
          |
       +--+---+
       |      |
       v      v
    Recovered Incomplete

A successful recovery request does not automatically establish:

    Recovered

The recovered interval must satisfy the required evidence checks.

### 9.5 Partial Recovery

Suppose an interruption covers a 30-second interval.

The exchange's REST endpoint returns only the latest 100 trades.

Those 100 trades may cover the final five seconds of the interruption.

The recovery request succeeded technically.

However, the earlier 25 seconds remain unresolved.

The correct classification is:

    Incomplete

The system must preserve the unresolved interval.

### 9.6 Coverage State Independence

Trade coverage state is independent of stream lifecycle.

For example:

    Stream Lifecycle = Active

    Trade Coverage = Incomplete

This is valid.

It means the stream is currently processing live executions while retaining an unresolved historical gap.

The Stream Registry must not automatically stop live processing merely because an earlier interval remains incomplete.

### 9.7 Coverage History

The processor may maintain a bounded in-memory summary of recent coverage transitions.

Longer-term coverage metadata belongs to the appropriate persistent operational or dataset-integrity subsystem.

Operational history must not be written into the market-data PostgreSQL tables merely for convenience.

## 10. Live Ingestion During Recovery

Trade recovery must not unnecessarily interrupt the acquisition of new live executions.

When a historical coverage gap is detected, MarketForge should continue receiving live trades while the Recovery Coordinator attempts to retrieve missing executions.

The Trade Processing subsystem must coordinate these two data sources without introducing duplicate publication or unsupported continuity guarantees.

### 10.1 Design Principle

Live acquisition and historical recovery are independent activities.

Conceptually:

    Exchange WebSocket
           |
           v
      Live Trades
           |
           v
    Trade Processor
           |
           +----------------------+
           |                      |
           v                      v
    Live Admission          Recovery Required
           |                      |
           |                      v
           |               Recovery Coordinator
           |                      |
           |                      v
           |               Historical Acquisition
           |                      |
           |                      v
           |               Recovered Trades
           |                      |
           +----------+-----------+
                      |
                      v
             Trade Reconciliation
                      |
                      v
              Canonical Publication

The diagram represents logical processing.

The implementation may use different internal pipelines depending on the selected recovery and publication policy.

### 10.2 Live Acquisition Must Continue

During recovery:

- The current WebSocket subscription remains active when healthy.
- Incoming trades continue through normal decoding and normalization.
- Native execution identities are preserved.
- Deduplication state remains available.
- Coverage state records the unresolved interval.
- Recovery work must not block the transport reader.
- Recovery requests must respect exchange rate limits.

The Recovery Coordinator must not close a healthy live connection merely to retrieve missing historical trades.

### 10.3 Recovery Publication Policies

Two publication strategies are supported.

**ContinueLive**

Publish new live executions immediately while recovering earlier intervals separately.

**HoldUntilReconciled**

Temporarily buffer new live executions until the recovery interval has been reconciled, then publish the combined sequence according to the configured ordering contract.

| Policy | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| ContinueLive | Minimal live publication latency | Recovered events may arrive after newer events |
| HoldUntilReconciled | Supports ordered recovery before publication | Introduces latency and bounded-buffer requirements |

The selected policy must be explicit.

### 10.4 Initial Default

The recommended initial policy is:

    ContinueLive

This preserves the primary purpose of MarketForge Live: delivering current exchange events with minimal avoidable delay.

However, the system must preserve coverage metadata.

A downstream consumer requiring complete event-time ordering must not interpret continued live publication as proof of historical completeness.

### 10.5 Strictly Ordered Consumers

Some downstream consumers may require an uninterrupted chronological sequence.

Examples include:

- Trade-count bars.
- Tick imbalance bars.
- Run bars.
- Execution-arrival analysis.
- Historical dataset construction.

Such consumers must not silently process a stream containing unresolved historical gaps as though it were complete.

Their subscription or processing contract must explicitly define whether they:

- Accept incomplete coverage.
- Pause until recovery completes.
- Require ordered replay.
- Reject incomplete intervals.

This behavior belongs to the downstream delivery and processing contract.

The Trade Processor reports the integrity condition but does not implement trading-feature algorithms.

## 11. Recovery Session Management

A recovery session represents one bounded attempt to reconcile an identified trade-coverage interval.

The Recovery Coordinator owns recovery scheduling and execution policy.

The Trade Reconciliation Engine owns execution identity matching and coverage evaluation.

### 11.1 RecoverySession

Conceptual representation:

    RecoverySession {
        recovery_id
        stream_id

        recovery_generation
        processing_generation
        subscription_generation

        interval_start
        interval_end

        first_known_identity
        last_known_identity

        acquisition_strategy
        publication_policy

        status
        started_at
        deadline
    }

This representation is illustrative.

The exact Rust types must reuse the domain contracts established in document 02.

### 11.2 Recovery Identity

Each recovery attempt receives a unique:

    RecoveryId

Repeated recovery attempts for the same logical interval must remain distinguishable.

For example:

    Recovery 15:
        Initial REST query

    Recovery 16:
        Retry with adjusted time range

    Recovery 17:
        Historical archive fallback

A previous recovery attempt must not overwrite the results of a newer authoritative reconciliation operation.

### 11.3 Recovery States

Supported states:

    Created
    PendingAdmission
    Acquiring
    Normalizing
    Reconciling
    ValidatingCoverage
    Completed
    Partial
    Failed
    Cancelled

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Created | Recovery request registered |
| PendingAdmission | Waiting for rate-limit or resource capacity |
| Acquiring | Historical executions being retrieved |
| Normalizing | Recovered records undergoing canonical normalization |
| Reconciling | Matching recovered and existing executions |
| ValidatingCoverage | Evaluating completeness evidence |
| Completed | Required coverage established |
| Partial | Some coverage recovered, unresolved intervals remain |
| Failed | Recovery attempt failed |
| Cancelled | Recovery attempt superseded or stopped |

A successful acquisition request does not automatically result in `Completed`.

### 11.4 Recovery Boundaries

A recovery session must define the interval being investigated.

Potential boundaries include:

    LastAcceptedTradeBeforeInterruption

    FirstAcceptedTradeAfterInterruption

    InterruptionDetectedAt

    ConnectionRestoredAt

The exact missing-execution interval may not be known.

Therefore, recovery requests should include an appropriate overlap when supported by the exchange.

### 11.5 Overlapping Recovery Intervals

Multiple recovery requests may overlap.

For example:

    Recovery A:
        12:00:00 — 12:00:10

    Recovery B:
        12:00:08 — 12:00:20

The coordinator should avoid redundant acquisition when possible.

However, overlapping recovery records must remain safe to reconcile.

The Trade Reconciliation Engine uses canonical execution identities to prevent duplicate acceptance.

### 11.6 Recovery Cancellation

A recovery session may be cancelled when:

- The stream is removed.
- The recovery request is superseded.
- The configuration becomes incompatible.
- The metadata revision cannot be resolved.
- Runtime shutdown begins.
- The user explicitly cancels recovery.

Cancellation must not cause already accepted live trades to disappear.

Recovered records that have not been committed remain unaccepted.

### 11.7 Recovery Generation Fencing

Every recovery completion must validate the current runtime context.

Required checks include:

    StreamId

    RecoveryGeneration

    ProcessingGeneration

    ConfigurationRevision

    MetadataRevision

A recovered trade may refer to an older connection or subscription generation.

That alone does not make the historical execution obsolete.

**Connection-generation fencing must not incorrectly discard valid historical executions retrieved after reconnection.**

Instead, recovery results are validated against the recovery session and the applicable historical normalization context.

## 12. Recovery Buffering

Some recovery publication policies require buffering live executions while historical acquisition is underway.

Buffers must be bounded and explicitly owned.

### 12.1 RecoveryBuffer

Conceptual representation:

    RecoveryBuffer {
        stream_id
        recovery_id

        maximum_records
        maximum_bytes
        maximum_duration

        buffered_trades
        first_received_at
        last_received_at
    }

The buffer stores normalized execution records or another lossless intermediate representation.

It must not become a permanent trade-history database.

### 12.2 Buffer Ordering

Buffered live trades preserve their source ordering.

For records from one WebSocket message, the native record order must also be preserved.

The buffer must not arbitrarily sort executions by timestamp before the reconciliation strategy determines the required ordering.

### 12.3 Buffer Capacity

Buffer capacity is constrained by:

    MaximumRecords
    MaximumBytes
    MaximumDuration

The first exceeded limit triggers the configured overflow policy.

Unlimited buffering is prohibited.

### 12.4 Buffer Overflow

If a strict hold-and-reconcile policy exhausts its buffer:

    Recovery Buffer Full
             |
             v
    Stop Strict Hold Admission
             |
             v
    Report Buffer Overflow
             |
             v
    Evaluate Fallback Policy

Possible fallback actions:

    SwitchToContinueLive

    SpillToBoundedDiskStorage

    MarkCoverageIncomplete

    EscalateProcessingFailure

The selected fallback must be configured.

A fallback must not silently weaken an existing subscriber's strict ordering guarantee.

If that guarantee can no longer be satisfied, the affected delivery contract must fail or explicitly transition to a degraded state.

### 12.5 Existing Live Retention Infrastructure

MarketForge already includes temporary live retention using memory and Parquet spill mechanisms.

Recovery buffering should reuse the underlying bounded storage primitives where appropriate.

However, the ownership semantics differ.

**Live retention** preserves acquired events for bootstrap and historical reconstruction.

**Recovery buffering** temporarily holds executions pending reconciliation or ordered publication.

The two components may share storage infrastructure without sharing lifecycle ownership.

### 12.6 Buffer Release

When reconciliation completes:

- Identify exact duplicates.
- Validate unresolved conflicts.
- Establish the publication sequence.
- Submit accepted executions.
- Update coverage state.
- Release temporary buffering resources.

A failed recovery must not silently discard buffered executions that have not yet reached their required publication or retention boundary.

## 13. Trade Reconciliation Engine

The Trade Reconciliation Engine compares recovered executions with already accepted live executions.

Its primary purpose is to determine which recovered records are new, which are duplicates, and whether the requested interval is complete.

### 13.1 Responsibilities

The engine must:

- Validate recovery-source compatibility.
- Normalize recovered executions.
- Resolve native execution identities.
- Compare recovered records against known live identities.
- Detect exact duplicates.
- Detect conflicting duplicates.
- Identify previously missing executions.
- Preserve event and source ordering metadata.
- Evaluate interval coverage.
- Report unresolved gaps.
- Produce an explicit reconciliation result.

The engine does not own REST clients, exchange retry policies, or global recovery scheduling.

### 13.2 Reconciliation Inputs

Inputs include:

    RecoverySession

    RecoveredTradeRecords

    ExistingAcceptedTradeIdentities

    InstrumentMetadataRevisions

    CompiledIdentityStrategy

    CoverageEvidence

The engine must not assume that every previously published execution remains in its bounded in-memory deduplication cache.

For recovery intervals extending beyond that cache, a compatible retained identity index or other durable reconciliation source may be necessary.

### 13.3 Reconciliation Pipeline

    Recovered Native Trades
               |
               v
    Validate Recovery Source
               |
               v
    Select Applicable Metadata Revision
               |
               v
    Normalize Recovered Trades
               |
               v
    Resolve Native Trade Identities
               |
               v
    Compare Against Accepted Identities
               |
         +-----+-----+
         |           |
         v           v
      Existing       New
         |           |
         v           v
      Compare      Candidate
      Content      Recovery
         |         Execution
         v
    Duplicate or Conflict
               |
               v
    Evaluate Coverage Evidence
               |
               v
    Reconciliation Result

The process must preserve enough information to reconstruct why an execution was accepted or rejected.

### 13.4 ReconciliationResult

Conceptual representation:

    ReconciliationResult {
        recovery_id
        stream_id

        new_executions
        exact_duplicates
        conflicting_executions
        rejected_records

        requested_interval
        verified_coverage
        unresolved_intervals

        completeness
        diagnostics
    }

The result must distinguish acquisition success from coverage completeness.

### 13.5 Exact Duplicate Reconciliation

If a recovered execution matches an already accepted native identity and compatible fingerprint:

    ExactDuplicate

The record must not be republished through the normal canonical live-event path.

The reconciliation result records the duplicate.

### 13.6 Conflicting Execution Reconciliation

If a recovered execution has the same identity but different economic content:

    TradeIdentityConflict

The engine must not silently replace the previously accepted trade.

Possible causes include:

- Incorrect REST/WebSocket identity mapping.
- Different aggregation semantics.
- Incorrect metadata revision.
- Exchange data correction.
- Malformed recovered payload.
- Incorrect normalization configuration.

The conflict must remain explicit until resolved by a supported policy.

### 13.7 New Recovered Executions

A recovered record absent from the known accepted identity set is a candidate new execution.

Before acceptance, the engine must verify:

- Identity validity.
- Source compatibility.
- Canonical normalization.
- Applicable metadata revision.
- Deduplication scope.
- Publication policy.
- Recovery interval membership.

A missing identity in a bounded cache alone is insufficient to prove that the execution was never published.

### 13.8 Aggregate Versus Individual Trades

Recovered and live sources may expose different trade granularity.

For example:

    Live:
        Aggregate Trade

    Recovery:
        Individual Executions

These records may represent overlapping economic activity without sharing identical execution identities.

The engine must not treat them as interchangeable.

Reconciliation requires an explicitly supported aggregation-aware strategy.

If no compatible strategy exists, the interval remains unresolved.

## 14. Recovery Coverage Verification

Recovery completeness must be established through evidence appropriate to the exchange's native trade protocol.

### 14.1 Coverage Verification Requirements

The engine evaluates:

- Requested recovery interval.
- Acquired record range.
- Pagination completeness.
- Native execution identifiers.
- Verified identifier continuity rules.
- Overlap with accepted live executions.
- Historical archive coverage.
- Unsupported or rejected records.
- Remaining unknown intervals.

### 14.2 Verified Continuity

A recovered interval may be classified as complete when the available evidence establishes that the required executions were retrieved.

Examples include:

- Exhaustive exchange-supported time-range pagination.
- Verified consecutive native execution identifiers.
- Historical archives with established coverage guarantees.
- A documented recovery mechanism proving the interval complete.

The exact standard depends on the exchange.

### 14.3 Limited Recent-Trade Endpoints

Some exchanges expose only a bounded number of recent executions.

For example:

    Maximum returned records = 100

A successful response containing 100 records does not establish coverage of an arbitrarily long outage.

If the requested interval extends beyond the available history, the recovery result must remain partial or incomplete.

### 14.4 Empty Recovery Results

An empty historical response does not automatically prove that no executions occurred.

The result may indicate:

- No trades in the requested interval.
- Incorrect query parameters.
- Unsupported history range.
- API retention limits.
- Exchange-side incomplete results.
- Pagination errors.

The recovery-source contract must determine whether an empty result is authoritative.

### 14.5 Time-Range Pagination

When an exchange supports time-range queries, the acquisition engine must verify that all required pages were retrieved.

A page limit may require splitting the interval into smaller requests.

The reconciliation engine must not infer completeness merely because the final request succeeded.

### 14.6 Identity-Based Recovery

If an exchange supports stable native execution-ID pagination, recovery may use those identifiers to traverse missing history.

However, an exchange's identifiers must be verified to support the required pagination semantics.

An opaque identifier must not be treated as an integer range without explicit protocol support.

### 14.7 Coverage Evidence

Conceptual representation:

    CoverageEvidence {
        source
        requested_interval
        retrieved_interval
        pagination_complete
        identity_continuity_verified
        overlap_verified
        unresolved_boundaries
    }

Evidence must be associated with the corresponding recovery session.

### 14.8 Partial Completeness

A recovery result may contain multiple verified and unverified subintervals.

Example:

    Requested:
        12:00:00 — 12:00:30

    Verified:
        12:00:20 — 12:00:30

    Unresolved:
        12:00:00 — 12:00:20

The engine must preserve this distinction.

The unresolved portion must not disappear merely because the most recent executions were recovered.

### 14.9 Recovery Completion

A recovery session may report:

    Complete
    Partial
    Unverified
    Failed

These outcomes are distinct from REST request success or failure.

Only a `Complete` outcome backed by sufficient evidence may establish verified recovery for the requested interval.

## 15. Trade Ordering

MarketForge Live must preserve the distinction between exchange-native execution order, transport arrival order, event-time order, and canonical publication order.

These orderings are not necessarily equivalent.

The Trade Processor must preserve source ordering while allowing recovery and downstream merging components to enforce additional ordering guarantees.

### 15.1 Ordering Dimensions

The initial implementation distinguishes:

| Ordering | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transport order | Order in which WebSocket frames are received |
| Record order | Position of an execution inside a native message |
| Native execution order | Ordering established by exchange-native identifiers |
| Event-time order | Ordering by exchange execution timestamp |
| Recovery order | Order in which historical executions are retrieved |
| Publication order | Order accepted canonical events are delivered |
| Cross-stream order | Ordering established by the separate merge subsystem |

These dimensions must not be conflated.

### 15.2 Native Arrival Order

The Trade Processor receives normalized executions in source order.

Conceptually:

    WebSocket Frame 100
        |
        +-- Trade A
        +-- Trade B

    WebSocket Frame 101
        |
        +-- Trade C

The initial processing order is:

    A → B → C

This preserves the ordering observed through the native transport.

However, transport order does not automatically establish exchange execution-time order.

### 15.3 Execution Timestamp Ordering

Exchange execution timestamps may be:

- Equal.
- Strictly increasing.
- Nondecreasing.
- Occasionally decreasing.
- Insufficiently precise to establish a total order.

The Trade Processor must not silently assume:

    current.event_timestamp >= previous.event_timestamp

unless the selected exchange protocol provides that guarantee.

Out-of-order execution timestamps must be preserved and reported according to the configured integrity policy.

### 15.4 Native Identifier Ordering

Some exchanges provide monotonic native trade identifiers.

Others provide opaque identifiers or IDs whose ordering semantics are insufficiently documented.

The compiled identity strategy determines whether native identifiers can be used for ordering.

Supported conceptual policies:

    NoOrderingGuarantee
    MonotonicIncreasing
    StrictlyIncreasing
    Consecutive

A native identifier must not become a universal ordering key merely because it is numeric.

### 15.5 Ordering Across Recovery

Recovered executions may precede already published live executions in event time.

Example:

    Live publication:
        Trade 100
        Trade 105
        Trade 106

    Recovery discovers:
        Trade 101
        Trade 102
        Trade 103
        Trade 104

The processor must not pretend these recovered executions originally arrived before Trade 105.

Their recovery provenance must remain available.

Whether they are published as late events or through an ordered replay depends on the selected publication contract.

### 15.6 Stable Tie-Breaking

When multiple accepted trades share the same event timestamp, the system may require deterministic tie-breaking.

The selected ordering strategy may use:

- Verified native execution ordering.
- Native record position.
- Transport receive sequence.
- Recovery source position.
- Stable execution identity.

Tie-breaking must be deterministic.

However, an implementation-defined tie-breaker does not establish true exchange execution order.

The resulting ordering guarantee must reflect that limitation.

### 15.7 No Global Ordering in the Trade Processor

The Trade Processor owns one logical source stream.

It must not attempt to establish global event-time ordering across:

- Multiple instruments.
- Multiple exchanges.
- Separate market families.
- Independent physical connections.

Cross-stream event-time ordering belongs to MarketForge's merge and reorder subsystem.

## 16. Canonical Publication Cursors

Every accepted canonical trade must be associated with a stable publication identity.

The source-stream cursor identifies the order in which MarketForge accepted and published events within one logical stream.

It is not an exchange-native execution identifier.

### 16.1 Cursor Purpose

Publication cursors support:

- Per-stream event ordering.
- Subscriber replay boundaries.
- Duplicate-publication prevention.
- Recovery reconciliation.
- Temporary retention indexing.
- Diagnostics.
- Consumer consistency checks.

The cursor contract must remain compatible with **02 — Domain Models and Contracts**.

### 16.2 StreamCursor

Conceptually:

    StreamCursor {
        stream_id
        cursor
    }

The cursor is monotonically increasing within its defined stream identity and cursor epoch.

If MarketForge uses a lifetime-stable cursor namespace, cursor allocation must survive runtime restart without reuse.

If restart-safe continuation cannot be guaranteed, a new cursor epoch must be explicitly identified.

### 16.3 Cursor Assignment

Cursors are assigned only after a trade has passed:

    Structural Validation
             |
             v
    Normalization
             |
             v
    Identity Validation
             |
             v
    Deduplication
             |
             v
    Integrity Acceptance
             |
             v
    Publication Admission
             |
             v
    Cursor Assignment

The implementation must not assign a normal published-event cursor to a rejected record.

Exact duplicates must not receive another published-event cursor.

### 16.4 Cursor Ordering

For one source stream:

    Cursor 100
        |
        v
    Cursor 101
        |
        v
    Cursor 102

Publication order must preserve cursor order.

A later accepted event must not overtake an earlier accepted event within the same publication lane.

### 16.5 Cursor Reservation and Commit

Cursor allocation must be coordinated with publication admission.

Conceptually:

    Accepted Trade
          |
          v
    Reserve Publication Capacity
          |
          v
    Assign Cursor
          |
          v
    Commit Event to Publication Queue
          |
          v
    Expose Accepted Cursor

The cursor and publication queue entry must become authoritative together.

If publication admission fails, the processor must not silently advance the accepted cursor while losing the corresponding event.

### 16.6 Cursor Gaps

A cursor gap may indicate:

- An explicitly defined reserved cursor.
- Missing published events.
- A consumer delivery gap.
- A restart boundary.
- A publication-system failure.

The meaning depends on the canonical cursor contract.

For the initial lossless publication design, unexplained gaps in a continuous cursor epoch are invalid.

The processor must not silently skip accepted-event cursors.

### 16.7 Native Trade IDs vs Cursors

Example:

    Native Trade ID: 987654321

    Stream Cursor: 1205

These identifiers have different responsibilities.

The native trade ID identifies an exchange execution.

The stream cursor identifies MarketForge's accepted publication position.

A recovered execution may have an older native trade ID but receive a newer stream cursor when published through the live lane.

This is valid under a publication-order cursor contract.

It does not imply that the recovered execution occurred later economically.

## 17. Recovery Replay and Publication

Recovery results must be integrated into canonical output according to an explicit publication strategy.

The Trade Reconciliation Engine determines which recovered executions are eligible for acceptance.

The Trade Processor and Publisher coordinate their delivery.

### 17.1 Publication Strategies

The initial supported strategies are:

    ContinueLive
    HoldUntilReconciled

These strategies were introduced in Section 10.

They define how live publication behaves while an earlier interval is being recovered.

### 17.2 ContinueLive

Under `ContinueLive`, new live executions continue to be published while recovery proceeds.

Example:

    Live:
        Trade 100
        Trade 105
        Trade 106

    Recovery:
        Trade 101
        Trade 102
        Trade 103
        Trade 104

A possible canonical publication sequence is:

    Cursor 1 -> Trade 100
    Cursor 2 -> Trade 105
    Cursor 3 -> Trade 106
    Cursor 4 -> Trade 101 [Recovered]
    Cursor 5 -> Trade 102 [Recovered]
    Cursor 6 -> Trade 103 [Recovered]
    Cursor 7 -> Trade 104 [Recovered]

This preserves publication order.

It does not preserve native execution order.

The event envelope must distinguish recovered executions from live-origin executions where the canonical provenance contract supports that distinction.

### 17.3 Late Recovered Events

Recovered executions published after newer live executions are late relative to the observed live publication frontier.

The system must preserve:

    Original Execution Timestamp
    Native Trade Identity
    Recovery Source
    Recovery Session
    Publication Cursor

The original execution timestamp must not be replaced with the recovery time.

### 17.4 HoldUntilReconciled

Under `HoldUntilReconciled`, the processor temporarily delays eligible live publication while attempting recovery.

Conceptually:

    Recovery Required
           |
           v
    Start Bounded Live Buffer
           |
           v
    Acquire Missing Trades
           |
           v
    Reconcile Identities
           |
           v
    Establish Ordered Sequence
           |
           v
    Publish Reconciled Trades
           |
           v
    Resume Normal Live Publication

This strategy introduces additional latency.

It must operate within explicit buffer and recovery deadlines.

### 17.5 Strict Ordering Requirements

`HoldUntilReconciled` must not automatically claim strict exchange execution order.

Strict ordering requires an authoritative ordering basis.

For example, if the exchange provides verified consecutive trade identifiers, the processor may be able to establish an exact native sequence.

If the exchange exposes only opaque identifiers and coarse timestamps, a unique execution order may remain unknowable.

In that case, the system must distinguish:

    DeterministicallyOrdered

from:

    ExchangeOrderVerified

The first does not imply the second.

### 17.6 Recovery Ordering

Recovered records may arrive from REST in reverse chronological order or in pages that overlap.

The reconciliation engine must normalize the retrieval order according to the documented recovery-source semantics.

It must not assume that the order returned by one REST response is necessarily the correct canonical execution order.

### 17.7 Recovery Replay Boundary

A recovery replay must establish a clear boundary between:

- Previously accepted executions.
- Newly recovered executions.
- Buffered live executions.
- Subsequent live executions.

The boundary must be associated with the current recovery session.

No execution may be accepted twice because it appears in more than one category.

### 17.8 Incomplete Recovery

If recovery cannot establish the required interval:

    Recovery Incomplete

The system must not silently release a strict hold as though continuity had been verified.

The configured fallback determines whether to:

- Continue live publication with incomplete coverage.
- Preserve buffered records for later processing.
- Fail the strict delivery lane.
- Escalate the affected stream.

The selected action must preserve the subscriber's declared delivery contract.

## 18. Subscriber Consistency

Subscribers consume canonical trades through the Publisher.

The Trade Processor must provide enough ordering and integrity metadata for the Publisher to implement consistent delivery.

### 18.1 Subscriber Requirements

A subscriber may require:

- Low-latency live execution delivery.
- Per-stream publication ordering.
- Coverage-status notifications.
- Recovered-event provenance.
- Replay from a known cursor.
- Duplicate-free execution delivery.
- Strict event-time ordering.

Not all requirements are simultaneously achievable for every exchange and recovery source.

The subscription contract must make the available guarantees explicit.

### 18.2 Delivery Profiles

Conceptual delivery profiles:

    LiveLowLatency
    PublicationOrdered
    RecoveryAware
    StrictContinuity

**LiveLowLatency**

Prioritizes delivery of currently received executions.

Historical recovery may appear later.

**PublicationOrdered**

Preserves MarketForge source-stream cursor order.

It does not imply event-time ordering.

**RecoveryAware**

Exposes recovery provenance and coverage-status changes.

**StrictContinuity**

Requires a verified continuity contract before declaring an interval complete.

Strict continuity may require delaying or suspending delivery.

These profiles are conceptual capabilities.

Their final API representation belongs to the Publisher specification.

### 18.3 Coverage Notifications

Coverage changes are operational integrity events.

Examples:

    TradeCoverageUnverified
    TradeGapSuspected
    TradeGapConfirmed
    TradeRecoveryStarted
    TradeRecoveryCompleted
    TradeRecoveryIncomplete

These events must be distinguishable from ordinary canonical executions.

A subscriber should not need to infer coverage failures from missing timestamps or native trade-ID jumps.

### 18.4 Recovery Provenance

A recovered trade should preserve:

    Source = REST / Archive / Other

    RecoveryId

    OriginalEventTimestamp

    NativeTradeIdentity

Where the canonical provenance model supports it, the event may also identify the original interrupted interval.

### 18.5 Duplicate Delivery

The normal canonical trade lane must not intentionally publish an exact duplicate native execution as a new event.

However, end-to-end duplicate-free delivery across publisher failures and consumer restarts requires additional delivery guarantees.

For example, if a consumer receives an event but its acknowledgement is lost, a replay mechanism may deliver that same event again.

Therefore, MarketForge must distinguish:

    Execution Deduplication

from:

    Subscriber Delivery Deduplication

The first is owned by the Trade Processor.

The second belongs to the Publisher and subscriber replay contract.

### 18.6 Slow Subscribers

A slow subscriber must not silently cause the Trade Processor to discard accepted trades.

The Publisher owns subscriber-level backpressure handling.

Possible actions include:

- Bounded subscriber queues.
- Replay from retained events.
- Subscriber disconnection.
- Explicit delivery-gap reporting.

A subscriber delivery failure must not be misclassified as an exchange trade-continuity failure.

### 18.7 Replay From Cursor

When temporary retention supports cursor-based replay, a subscriber may request events beginning from a known publication position.

The Publisher must verify that the requested cursor remains available.

If the requested interval has expired from retention, the Publisher must report that replay is unavailable.

It must not silently begin at a later cursor while claiming uninterrupted delivery.

## 19. Ordering and Temporary Retention

MarketForge's Live Retention Manager stores accepted market events temporarily for downstream bootstrap and dataset construction.

The Trade Processor does not own this storage.

### 19.1 Retention Relationship

Conceptually:

    Trade Processor
          |
          v
    Canonical Accepted Trades
          |
          v
    Publisher / Event Distribution
          |
          +---------------------+
          |                     |
          v                     v
    Live Subscribers      Live Retention
                                |
                                v
                         Memory Buffer
                                |
                                v
                         Parquet Spill

The actual distribution topology is defined in the Publisher and Live Retention specifications.

### 19.2 Retained Event Identity

Retained trades must preserve:

- Canonical event identity.
- Source-stream cursor.
- Native trade identity.
- Exchange event timestamp.
- Source provenance.
- Configuration revision.
- Applicable metadata revision.

These fields allow retained events to be reconciled with live and historical acquisition.

### 19.3 Event-Time Ordering

Temporary retention may contain events whose execution timestamps are not monotonically increasing.

This is expected under:

    ContinueLive

when historical recovery inserts late executions into publication order.

Retention must preserve the distinction between:

    Publication Order

and:

    Event-Time Order

A later offline processing stage may establish event-time ordering using explicit rules.

It must not reinterpret publication cursors as execution timestamps.

### 19.4 Recovery and Retention

Recovered trades accepted into the canonical stream may also enter temporary retention.

The system must prevent exact duplicate native executions from being retained as independent accepted events.

However, this does not require the Trade Processor to maintain an unbounded historical identity index.

Recovery reconciliation may consult compatible retained identities when necessary.

### 19.5 Retention Coverage

Retention must preserve coverage metadata sufficient to identify:

- Unverified intervals.
- Confirmed gaps.
- Partially recovered intervals.
- Fully verified intervals.
- Publication discontinuities.

An event file containing valid canonical trades is not automatically a complete dataset.

### 19.6 Crash Consistency

If the runtime crashes after accepting an event but before durable retention, the event may be unavailable after restart.

The system must therefore distinguish:

    AcceptedIntoRuntime

    PublishedToSubscriber

    RetainedInMemory

    DurablyPersisted

These are different completion boundaries.

An end-to-end exactly-once durability guarantee requires a separate transactional or replayable publication contract.

The Trade Processor alone cannot provide that guarantee.

### 19.7 Restart and Cursor Continuity

After restart, the runtime must not reuse accepted publication identities in a way that makes distinct executions indistinguishable.

The system must either:

- Restore a validated persistent cursor frontier.
- Establish a new explicit cursor epoch.
- Use another documented restart-safe identity scheme.

The Publisher and retention layer must use the same cursor contract.

## 20. Publication Invariants

The following invariants apply to the accepted canonical trade stream.

### 20.1 Execution Identity

    One native execution identity must not be
    published twice as independent accepted trades
    within the verified deduplication scope.

### 20.2 Cursor Identity

    Every accepted published event has one
    publication identity.

    Publication identities are not reused within
    their defined cursor namespace.

### 20.3 Publication Ordering

    Per-stream publication order follows the
    committed cursor order.

### 20.4 Event-Time Preservation

    Recovery does not replace original execution
    timestamps with acquisition or publication time.

### 20.5 Provenance

    Live and recovered executions retain
    distinguishable acquisition provenance.

### 20.6 Coverage

    An active stream may have incomplete
    historical coverage.

    Connectivity does not imply completeness.

### 20.7 Recovery

    Recovery completion requires verified
    coverage evidence.

    Successful acquisition alone is insufficient.

### 20.8 Subscriber Delivery

    Subscriber delivery failures must not be
    silently classified as native trade loss.

### 20.9 Retention

    Temporary retention does not automatically
    imply durable, complete historical coverage.

### 20.10 Generation Safety

    Obsolete live processing generations cannot
    publish new authoritative events.

    Historical recovery records remain eligible
    only through a valid recovery session.

## 21. Trade Processing Resource Management

The Trade Processing subsystem must operate within explicit resource limits while supporting high-throughput execution streams.

Unlike the L2 BookStore, the Trade Processor does not retain the complete historical sequence of executions.

It maintains only the runtime state required for:

- Current execution processing.
- Native identity deduplication.
- Continuity tracking.
- Recovery reconciliation.
- Publication coordination.
- Operational diagnostics.

Long-term and temporary event retention belong to their respective storage components.

### 21.1 Managed Resources

| Resource | Owner | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| Trade processor | Processing Runtime | Processing generation |
| Compiled trade format | Configuration Compiler | Configuration revision |
| Instrument context | Instrument Context Registry | Metadata revision |
| Deduplication cache | Trade Processor | Processing and recovery requirements |
| Continuity state | Trade Processor | Logical stream lifetime |
| Recovery session | Recovery Coordinator | Recovery operation |
| Recovery reconciliation state | Trade Reconciliation Engine | Recovery session |
| Recovery buffer | Recovery publication component | Recovery session |
| Inbound trade queue | Processing Runtime | Processing generation |
| Canonical publication queue | Publisher | Publisher lifetime |
| Temporary event retention | Live Retention Manager | Retention policy |
| Operational measurements | Metrics Registry | Runtime lifetime |

Every resource must have explicit acquisition, ownership, and release semantics.

### 21.2 Resource Limits

The initial implementation must support configurable limits for:

    MaximumActiveTradeProcessors

    MaximumInboundQueueMessages

    MaximumInboundQueueBytes

    MaximumDeduplicationEntries

    MaximumDeduplicationMemory

    MaximumRecoveryBufferMessages

    MaximumRecoveryBufferBytes

    MaximumConcurrentRecoverySessions

    MaximumPendingPublicationEvents

Limits must be enforced before resource exhaustion occurs.

The effective limits may be constrained by global runtime policies.

### 21.3 Per-Stream Isolation

Trade processors must maintain independent execution state.

For example:

    BTCUSDT Trades
          |
          v
    BTCUSDT Processor

    ETHUSDT Trades
          |
          v
    ETHUSDT Processor

A slow processor must not hold a global processing lock that blocks unrelated instruments.

Independent trade streams may execute concurrently.

However, updates to one logical stream's authoritative processing state must remain serialized.

### 21.4 Bounded Processing Queues

Each processor receives native or normalized trade records through a bounded queue.

The queue must preserve the required source ordering.

When processing throughput is lower than acquisition throughput:

    Incoming Trades
          |
          v
    Queue Occupancy Increases
          |
          v
    Backpressure Threshold
          |
          v
    Apply Flow-Control Policy

The system must not silently discard trade records to maintain throughput.

### 21.5 Queue Overflow

If the processing queue cannot safely accept additional records:

- Record the overload condition.
- Stop claiming uninterrupted acquisition.
- Preserve previously accepted records according to their ownership contract.
- Notify the appropriate runtime supervisor.
- Mark affected coverage as unverified when continuity can no longer be established.
- Trigger the configured overload or recovery procedure.

The Trade Processor must not silently skip incoming executions.

### 21.6 Memory Pressure

Memory pressure may originate from:

- Deduplication state.
- Recovery buffers.
- Large trade batches.
- Pending publication events.
- Processing queues.
- Retained normalization contexts.

The subsystem should expose component-specific memory usage.

Memory pressure must not trigger arbitrary eviction of records that have already been accepted but not yet transferred to their required publication or retention boundary.

### 21.7 Deduplication Memory

The deduplication cache is explicitly bounded.

When it reaches capacity, the configured eviction strategy removes eligible historical identities.

Eviction affects the available duplicate-detection window.

It must not be reported as loss of previously accepted canonical events.

The processor must expose the effective deduplication coverage available after eviction.

### 21.8 Recovery Resource Admission

Recovery sessions consume additional memory, processing capacity, and external API budgets.

The Recovery Coordinator must enforce admission limits independently of normal live processing.

Recovery workload must not consume all resources required for ongoing acquisition.

When resources are insufficient, recovery may be deferred.

Live processing should continue when its integrity and delivery contracts permit it.

### 21.9 Resource Cleanup

Stopping a processor must release:

- Inbound queue ownership.
- Pending normalization work.
- Deduplication state no longer required.
- Recovery buffer references.
- Processing-generation handles.
- Instrument context references.
- Pending control-plane operation references.

Resources still required by active recovery or retention components must remain owned by those components until their operations finish.

Cleanup must be idempotent.

## 22. Backpressure and Failure Isolation

Backpressure must be treated as a correctness concern.

MarketForge's preference for lossless market-data acquisition requires explicit behavior when processing cannot keep up.

### 22.1 Backpressure Boundaries

The processing path contains several independent capacity boundaries.

    WebSocket Reader
           |
           v
    Transport Queue
           |
           v
    Stream Routing Queue
           |
           v
    Trade Processing Queue
           |
           v
    Canonical Publication Queue
           |
           v
    Publisher / Retention

Each queue has its own owner and capacity.

A successful write into one queue does not guarantee successful delivery through every downstream stage.

### 22.2 Backpressure Propagation

When a downstream queue becomes full, the producer must follow the configured backpressure contract.

The initial preference is:

    Preserve Accepted Work
           |
           v
    Apply Bounded Backpressure
           |
           v
    Escalate Before Uncontrolled Loss

Blocking may temporarily propagate pressure upstream.

However, TCP flow control does not guarantee indefinite exchange-side buffering.

Therefore, blocking alone does not establish lossless acquisition under sustained overload.

### 22.3 Publication Backpressure

A trade may be successfully normalized but not yet admitted into the canonical publication queue.

The processor must distinguish:

    Normalized

    IntegrityAccepted

    PublicationCommitted

The accepted publication cursor must correspond to a committed event in the publication pipeline.

If publication capacity is unavailable, the processor must not silently discard the trade after advancing its authoritative cursor.

### 22.4 Recovery Backpressure

Recovery may produce a large batch of historical executions while the live stream remains active.

The system must prevent recovered records from starving live processing indefinitely.

Possible controls include:

- Independent bounded queues.
- Recovery admission limits.
- Cooperative processing budgets.
- Fair scheduling.
- Recovery batching.

The exact scheduling policy belongs to the Processing Runtime.

### 22.5 Slow Recovery Sources

Slow REST or archive acquisition must not block the WebSocket reader.

The Recovery Coordinator performs acquisition asynchronously.

The Trade Reconciliation Engine receives completed or incrementally acquired recovery records through a bounded interface.

### 22.6 Failure Isolation

A processing failure affecting one logical trade stream must not automatically terminate unrelated streams.

Example:

    Binance BTCUSDT Trades
             |
             v
    Normalization Failure
             |
             v
    BTCUSDT Processor Degraded

Other streams sharing the same physical connection may continue operating.

A connection-wide protocol failure may affect multiple streams, but that condition is handled by the Connection Manager and Subscription Manager.

### 22.7 No Silent Loss

The fundamental rule is:

    No Silent Trade Dropping

This does not mean that every external exchange event can always be recovered.

It means that MarketForge must explicitly report when it cannot preserve or establish the required acquisition and delivery guarantees.

## 23. Stream Lifecycle Integration

The Trade Processor follows the logical stream lifecycle managed by the Stream Registry.

It must not maintain an independent desired-state authority.

### 23.1 Activation

Activation requires:

- Valid exchange profile.
- Valid trade-format specification.
- Valid instrument context.
- Compiled normalization strategy.
- Configured native identity strategy.
- Allocated processing resources.
- Established message routing.
- Current subscription generation.

The processor becomes ready before native messages are allowed to enter an unbuffered processing path.

### 23.2 Active Processing

While active, the processor:

- Accepts messages from the current generation.
- Normalizes records.
- Validates identities.
- Detects duplicates.
- Updates continuity state.
- Coordinates publication admission.
- Reports operational health.

Its processing state remains distinct from the Stream Registry's logical lifecycle state.

### 23.3 Connection Loss

After connection loss:

    Connection Manager
           |
           v
    Connection Failure Event
           |
           v
    Subscription Manager
           |
           v
    Stream Registry
           |
           v
    Trade Processor
           |
           v
    Record Coverage Boundary

The Trade Processor must stop accepting obsolete-generation messages.

It retains relevant integrity state according to its configured retention policy.

A transport reconnection does not erase unresolved trade-coverage gaps.

### 23.4 Subscription Restoration

After subscription restoration, a new processing generation may be established.

The processor must:

- Validate the new subscription assignment.
- Preserve compatible deduplication history.
- Reestablish source routing.
- Resume live acquisition.
- Maintain unresolved coverage intervals.
- Coordinate recovery when required.

The logical StreamId remains unchanged.

### 23.5 Processor Restart

A processor may restart independently of its physical connection if the runtime architecture supports safe routing suspension and restoration.

A restart must not create an untracked interval during which native messages are silently lost.

Possible approaches include:

    Bounded Message Buffering

    Controlled Resubscription

    Explicit Coverage Invalidation

The selected behavior must be consistent with the stream's integrity contract.

### 23.6 Subscription Replacement

Changing native subscription parameters may require processor replacement.

Examples:

- Switching trade channel.
- Changing native instrument.
- Changing market classification.
- Changing trade granularity.
- Changing normalization-critical channel semantics.

A replacement must establish explicit old and new processing generations.

Previously accepted executions must not be published twice because two generations temporarily overlap.

### 23.7 Deactivation

On deactivation:

- Stop admitting new records.
- Fence the active processing generation.
- Apply the configured drain policy.
- Resolve pending publication commitments.
- Cancel or transfer recovery work according to policy.
- Preserve required coverage diagnostics.
- Release processing resources.

The processor must not independently reactivate itself.

### 23.8 Removal

After removal:

    StreamId = Removed

No delayed normalization completion, recovery result, or subscription event may recreate an authoritative processor for that removed stream.

Removal must be idempotent.

## 24. Configuration and Metadata Revisions

Trade processing uses immutable compiled normalization contexts.

The applicable metadata and configuration revisions must remain identifiable for every accepted execution.

### 24.1 Configuration Revision

Each processing generation references:

    ConfigurationRevision

The revision identifies:

- Native field extraction.
- Timestamp normalization.
- Quantity normalization.
- Aggressor-side interpretation.
- Identity strategy.
- Integrity rules.

A normalization-critical configuration change requires controlled processor replacement.

### 24.2 Metadata Revision

Each processor references an immutable:

    InstrumentMetadataRevision

This revision identifies the instrument economics used for normalization.

Relevant fields include:

    quantity_type
    contract_value
    contract_value_asset
    base_asset
    quote_asset

These values are supplied by Python orchestration or a validated local metadata snapshot.

No per-event PostgreSQL queries are permitted.

### 24.3 Revision Replacement

A metadata change may require rebuilding the compiled trade normalizer.

Conceptually:

    Current Context Revision N
              |
              v
    Receive Revision N+1
              |
              v
    Validate New Metadata
              |
              v
    Compile Replacement Context
              |
              v
    Establish Safe Event Boundary
              |
              v
    Activate New Context

No trade record may be partially normalized under two metadata revisions.

### 24.4 Historical Recovery Revisions

Recovered executions may precede the currently active metadata revision.

Example:

    Execution time: 10:00

    Contract specification changed: 12:00

    Recovery performed: 15:00

The recovered execution must use the metadata revision applicable to its event time when that revision can be established.

The processor must not automatically apply the latest contract multiplier.

### 24.5 Unknown Effective Time

A metadata revision may have a known acquisition timestamp but an unknown exchange-effective timestamp.

In such cases, MarketForge must preserve the uncertainty.

It must not fabricate the effective boundary.

Recovered executions requiring an unresolved historical revision may remain quarantined or unverified until the applicable context is established.

### 24.6 Identity Stability Across Revisions

Native execution identity remains independent of metadata revision.

However, normalized economic fingerprints may change if incorrect revisions are used.

Reconciliation must compare executions using compatible normalization semantics.

A metadata mismatch must not automatically be classified as an exchange execution conflict.

## 25. Operational Failure Handling

Trade-processing failures must be classified by their consequences.

The subsystem distinguishes:

- Record-level failures.
- Batch-level failures.
- Processing-state failures.
- Continuity failures.
- Publication failures.
- Recovery failures.
- Configuration failures.

### 25.1 Failure Classification

| Failure | Initial response |
|---|---|
| Malformed standalone trade | Reject or quarantine; evaluate coverage |
| Missing required field | Reject record; evaluate coverage |
| Invalid decimal | Reject record |
| Unknown instrument | Fail normalization |
| Unsupported quantity semantics | Fail configuration |
| Invalid aggressor side | Reject record |
| Missing native identity | Apply configured identity policy |
| Exact duplicate | Suppress duplicate publication |
| Conflicting duplicate | Quarantine and escalate |
| Native continuity violation | Record gap and request recovery |
| Connection interruption | Mark coverage unverified |
| Recovery incomplete | Preserve unresolved interval |
| Processing queue overflow | Escalate and invalidate affected coverage |
| Publication queue failure | Preserve or fail publication commitment explicitly |
| Obsolete generation | Reject stale operation |
| Incompatible metadata revision | Prevent invalid normalization |

A record-level failure may have stream-level continuity consequences.

The processor must not automatically continue reporting complete coverage after rejecting a potentially missing execution.

### 25.2 Record-Level Errors

A malformed independent execution may be rejected without terminating the entire logical trade stream.

However, the rejected execution may represent an unrecoverable hole in the acquired record sequence.

The integrity state must reflect that possibility.

### 25.3 Batch-Level Errors

Some exchange messages contain multiple executions.

Example:

    Native Message
        |
        +-- Trade A
        +-- Trade B
        +-- Trade C

If Trade B is malformed, the processor must follow an explicit batch policy.

Possible strategies:

    RejectEntireBatch

    AcceptValidRecordsAndMarkCoverage

    QuarantineEntireBatch

The strategy must respect native record independence and source ordering.

The processor must not silently omit Trade B and claim complete coverage.

### 25.4 Processor Failure

A nonrecoverable processor failure may require:

- Fencing the current generation.
- Stopping record admission.
- Preserving accepted publication commitments.
- Reporting the last valid processing boundary.
- Releasing resources.
- Requesting processor replacement or stream failure escalation.

The Stream Registry remains authoritative for the final lifecycle transition.

### 25.5 Repeated Normalization Failures

Repeated malformed records may indicate:

- Exchange protocol changes.
- Incorrect format configuration.
- Unsupported native message variants.
- Incorrect instrument metadata.
- Exchange-side data corruption.

The processor should support configurable escalation thresholds.

It must not continuously discard malformed records while reporting the stream as healthy.

### 25.6 Recovery Failure

Recovery failure does not necessarily require stopping current live acquisition.

If live processing remains healthy, the stream may continue with:

    Lifecycle = Active

    Coverage = Incomplete

The Recovery Coordinator determines retry and escalation behavior.

### 25.7 Permanent Configuration Failure

Examples:

- Unsupported raw quantity denomination.
- Missing contract metadata.
- Invalid field selectors.
- Unsupported timestamp representation.
- Incompatible canonical output schema.

These failures require correction.

They must not enter unlimited automatic retry loops.

    Previous ID = 100

    Current ID = 105

Expected:

    No Exact Missing-Trade Count

The processor may record a diagnostic observation.

It must not infer that four executions were lost.

### 30.3 Opaque Identifiers

Given:

    execId = "a8f3"

    execId = "b4e1"

Verify that the processor:

- Preserves both identities.
- Supports deduplication.
- Does not infer numerical ordering.
- Does not calculate missing-execution counts.

### 30.4 Timestamp Discontinuities

Simulate:

    Trade A at 12:00:00

    Trade B at 12:00:30

Expected:

    No Automatic ConfirmedGap

The processor may request additional diagnostics according to its configured policy.

### 30.5 Connection Interruption

Simulate:

    Last accepted trade
             |
             v
    Connection failure
             |
             v
    Connection restored
             |
             v
    First new live trade

Verify:

- The interruption boundary is recorded.
- Coverage becomes unverified where appropriate.
- Live processing resumes.
- Previously accepted trades remain accepted.
- Recovery is requested according to policy.
- Connection restoration does not establish historical completeness.

### 30.6 Processor Failure

Simulate processing failure while the WebSocket remains connected.

Verify that unprocessed or lost acquisition intervals are reported explicitly.

A healthy transport must not conceal a failed trade processor.

## 31. Recovery and Reconciliation Tests

Recovery tests must cover overlap, pagination, incomplete history, and conflicts between live and historical sources.

### 31.1 Complete Recovery

Simulate:

    Before interruption:
        Trades 100–110

    During interruption:
        Trades 111–119

    After reconnection:
        Trades 120–130

A verified recovery source returns:

    Trades 111–119

Expected:

- All nine missing executions are accepted once.
- Existing trades remain unchanged.
- Native identities are preserved.
- Recovery provenance is recorded.
- Coverage becomes verified for the recovered interval.

This result requires a fixture whose continuity guarantees support the completeness conclusion.

### 31.2 Partial Recovery

Simulate a missing interval containing 500 executions.

The recovery endpoint returns only the latest 100.

Expected:

    Recovery Result = Partial

The unresolved interval must remain recorded.

A successful HTTP response must not produce `Recovered` status without supporting coverage evidence.

### 31.3 Recovery Overlap

Simulate a recovery result containing:

    Previously accepted trades
    +
    Missing executions
    +
    Newly received live trades

Verify:

- Exact duplicates are suppressed.
- Conflicting duplicates are reported.
- New recovered executions are accepted.
- Publication policy is respected.
- Coverage evidence is updated.

### 31.4 Recovery Pagination

Verify:

- Multiple REST pages.
- Overlapping pages.
- Reverse chronological pages.
- Empty intermediate pages.
- Pagination termination.
- Endpoint retention limits.
- Query time-range boundaries.
- Rate-limit deferral.
- Recovery cancellation.

The engine must not claim completeness when required pages are missing.

### 31.5 Live Ingestion During Recovery

Start a recovery session while continuously injecting new WebSocket trades.

Verify that:

- The WebSocket reader remains unblocked.
- Live records continue to be normalized.
- Deduplication remains correct.
- Recovery work uses bounded resources.
- Current live coverage is tracked independently of historical recovery.
- Recovery results do not corrupt current processing generations.

### 31.6 Recovery Generation Safety

Simulate:

    Recovery Session 7 started

    Recovery Session 8 supersedes it

    Session 8 completes

    Session 7 returns late

The obsolete recovery completion must not overwrite the newer reconciliation result.

Historical records from a valid recovery session must not be rejected merely because they originated before the current WebSocket connection generation.

### 31.7 Aggregation Compatibility

Simulate:

    Live:
        Aggregated executions

    Recovery:
        Individual executions

Verify that the reconciliation engine does not equate the two representations without an explicitly supported mapping.

If the relationship cannot be established, coverage remains unresolved.

## 32. Publication and Ordering Tests

### 32.1 Source Ordering

### 32.2 Publication Cursors

Verify:

- Accepted trades receive unique cursors.
- Exact duplicates do not receive new cursors.
- Rejected records do not receive accepted-event cursors.
- Publication order follows cursor order.
- Cursor allocation is coordinated with publication admission.
- Cursor overflow is handled explicitly.
- Restart does not reuse an incompatible cursor identity.

### 32.3 ContinueLive

Simulate live publication followed by late recovery results.

Expected:

    Live event A
    Live event D
    Recovered event B
    Recovered event C

Publication cursors reflect acceptance order.

Execution timestamps preserve the original economic event times.

### 32.4 HoldUntilReconciled

### 32.5 Strict Ordering Failure

## 33. Fault-Injection Tests

The Trade Processor must remain correct when individual processing stages fail.

### 33.1 Failure Scenarios

Inject:

- Malformed JSON.
- Missing required fields.
- Invalid native trade identity.
- Incorrect instrument.
- Invalid decimal quantity.
- Invalid timestamp.
- Unknown aggressor side.
- Conflicting duplicate identity.
- Processor cancellation.
- Processing queue overflow.
- Recovery buffer overflow.
- Publication queue failure.
- Metadata revision mismatch.
- Configuration replacement during processing.
- Connection loss during recovery.
- Runtime shutdown during reconciliation.

Every failure must produce an explicit, testable outcome.

### 33.2 Batch Atomicity

For exchange messages containing multiple trade records, test the configured batch failure policy.

Possible outcomes include:

    RejectEntireBatch

    AcceptValidRecordsAndMarkCoverage

    QuarantineEntireBatch

The implementation must not silently skip malformed executions and claim complete coverage.

### 33.3 Failure Isolation

A failure in one trade processor must not terminate unrelated processors unless the underlying failure affects shared infrastructure.

Example:

    BTCUSDT Trade Processor Failed

must not automatically terminate:

    ETHUSDT Trade Processor

The Processing Runtime coordinates isolation and restart behavior.

### 33.4 Resource Cleanup

After cancellation, failure, or removal, verify that:

- Processing tasks terminate.
- Bounded queues are released.
- Deduplication allocations are released when eligible.
- Recovery operations are cancelled or transferred.
- Publication commitments receive explicit outcomes.
- Instrument context references are released.
- Obsolete generations cannot resume processing.

## 34. Property-Based Testing

Property-based tests should generate arbitrary valid event sequences and runtime operations.

### 34.1 Identity Invariants

    One accepted execution identity cannot produce
    multiple independent accepted trades within
    the verified deduplication scope.

    Conflicting identities are never silently overwritten.

    Connection generations do not alter economic identity.

    Opaque identifiers are never interpreted as
    sequential integers without a configured guarantee.

### 34.2 Cursor Invariants

    Accepted publication identities are unique.

    Per-stream publication order follows cursor order.

    Rejected records do not receive accepted cursors.

    Exact duplicates do not receive new accepted cursors.

    Publication failures cannot silently advance
    the authoritative accepted cursor.

### 34.3 Coverage Invariants

    Connectivity does not imply completeness.

    Successful recovery acquisition does not imply
    complete interval coverage.

    Confirmed gaps require supported evidence.

    Suspected gaps are not counted as confirmed loss.

    Unresolved coverage intervals remain identifiable.

### 34.4 Generation Invariants

    Obsolete processing generations cannot publish
    new authoritative live events.

    Obsolete recovery operations cannot overwrite
    newer authoritative reconciliation state.

    Valid historical recovery records are evaluated
    through recovery-session identity.

### 34.5 Resource Invariants

    Processing queues remain bounded.

    Deduplication memory remains bounded.

    Recovery buffers remain bounded.

    Cancelled operations release owned resources.

    Removed streams cannot be resurrected by
    delayed asynchronous operations.

### 34.6 Reference Model

Generated operations may include:

    ConnectionRestored

    Remove

For equivalent inputs and policies, the reference and optimized implementations must produce equivalent:

- Accepted execution identities.

- Recovery outcomes.

## 36. Implementation Acceptance Criteria

The Trade Processing subsystem is complete when the following requirements are satisfied.

### 36.1 Architecture

- Trade processing remains exchange-agnostic.

- Recovered and live identities can be reconciled.

### 36.5 Resource Safety

- Obsolete generations cannot resume processing.

### 36.6 Observability

- Recovery outcomes are measurable.
- Processing latency is measurable.

## 37. Final Architectural Contract

The Trade Processing and Continuity subsystem converts normalized exchange-reported executions into an integrity-aware canonical trade stream.

It guarantees:

- Exact preservation of native execution identities.
- Correct individual and aggregated trade semantics.
- Deterministic deduplication within the supported identity state.
- Explicit classification of continuity uncertainty.
- Generation-safe live processing.
- Recovery-aware reconciliation.
- Bounded resource consumption.
- Consistent publication identities.
- Preservation of original event timestamps.
- Explicit reporting of unrecoverable coverage gaps.

It does not guarantee:

- Complete trade history from every public WebSocket feed.
- Recovery beyond an exchange's available historical coverage.
- Exact missing-trade counts without native sequence guarantees.
- Global event-time ordering across independent streams.
- Unlimited deduplication history.
- Exactly-once delivery across arbitrary subscriber failures.
- Durable retention without the corresponding persistence guarantees.

The ownership chain is:

    Stream Registry
          |
          v
    Subscription Manager
          |
          v
    Connection Manager
          |
          v
    Exchange Protocol Adapter
          |
          v
    Trade Normalization
          |
          v
    Trade Processor
          |
          v
    Identity and Continuity Validation
          |
          v
    Canonical Trade Acceptance
          |
          v
    Publisher / Optional Recording

Recovery follows a coordinated path:

    Trade Continuity Tracker
              |
              v
    Recovery Coordinator
              |
              v
    Historical Trade Acquisition
              |
              v
    Trade Normalization
              |
              v
    Trade Reconciliation Engine
              |
              v
    Canonical Trade Acceptance

The Recovery Coordinator owns recovery scheduling and escalation.

The Trade Reconciliation Engine owns recovered-execution identity matching and coverage evaluation.

The Publisher owns subscriber delivery.

The Live Retention Manager owns temporary memory and Parquet retention.

The Recording Manager owns optional PostgreSQL market-data persistence.

These ownership boundaries must remain explicit throughout implementation.
